Take Action

The best time to question a surveillance contract isn't after it's renewed. It's before. Find out what your community agreed to, when the next decision happens, and who has the power to change it.

“The price of liberty is eternal vigilance.”

American political maxim · early 19th century

Start with the contract.

Before arguing about whether surveillance technology is good or bad, find out what your community actually agreed to. Ask for the current contract, amendments, purchase orders, renewal documents, and any terms or policies incorporated into the agreement.

Then find the dates that matter. When does the contract expire? Does it renew automatically? How much notice is required to stop a renewal? The deadline to act may come months before the contract itself expires.

READ THE FINE PRINT

Look beyond the price tag.

The most important terms may have little to do with the purchase price. Look for rules about data retention, outside-agency access, statewide or national lookup, audit requirements, evidence preservation, termination rights, and who has authority to change system settings.

If officials say the data is local, not shared, deleted after a certain period, or limited to certain investigations, ask where that restriction appears in the contract or written policy.

FIND THE DECISION MAKERS

Know who has the authority to renew it.

A police department may use the system, but that does not always mean the department has final authority over the contract. Find out whether renewal, expansion, funding, or policy changes require approval from a city council, county board, police commission, mayor, purchasing office, or another public body.

Once you know who decides, find out when they meet and how public comment works. Showing up after the decision is made is too late.

USE YOUR VOICE

Get involved before the vote.

Contact the elected officials responsible for the decision. Ask questions in writing. Attend public meetings. Speak during public comment. If a renewal or expansion is approaching, ask that it be discussed publicly before any new agreement is signed.

You do not need to be an expert on surveillance technology. Ask officials to explain what they are buying, what the contract allows, what safeguards are enforceable, and why the community should accept those terms.

ACTION CHECKLIST

Before the next decision, make sure you know these.

01 Current contract

Get the agreement, amendments, incorporated terms, and any renewal documents.

02 Renewal deadline

Find the expiration date, automatic-renewal language, and required notice period.

03 Public cost

Identify the purchase price, recurring costs, grants, and any proposed expansion spending.

04 Data rules

Check retention, sharing, search permissions, audit requirements, and evidence preservation.

05 Decision maker

Find out who can renew, expand, fund, terminate, or change the agreement.

06 Next public meeting

Know when the responsible body meets and how public comment works.

FIVE QUESTIONS TO ASK

Every community should be able to answer these.

  1. When does the current contract expire?
  2. When must notice be given to prevent automatic renewal?
  3. Who can access or search the data?
  4. What happens to the data after it is collected?
  5. Who gets to decide whether the system continues?

SPREAD THE WORD

Share the evidence, not just the opinion.

When you find the contract, renewal date, policies, or other public records, share them. Send them to neighbors, local journalists, community groups, and elected officials. Give other people the information they need to reach their own conclusions.

Public records are more powerful when the public actually sees them.
