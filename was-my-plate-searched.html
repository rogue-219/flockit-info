WAS MY PLATE SEARCHED?

A camera sighting is not the same as a search.

Flock cameras may record passing vehicles automatically. That does not necessarily mean anyone searched for your plate, viewed your vehicle history, or investigated you. Those are different events — and the records that could answer the question may be different too.

First, separate the two questions.

Have I Been Flocked? collects Flock search audit logs that have been obtained through public-records requests. You can enter your plate number and see whether it appears in the search records currently included in that database.

A match means the plate appeared in a disclosed search record. It does not necessarily mean you were suspected of a crime.

No match does not prove your plate was never searched. The database is incomplete because it can only include records that have been obtained and made available.

It also cannot tell you every time a Flock camera automatically recorded your vehicle. A camera sighting and a user search are different events.

Your vehicle may have been captured by a camera simply because it passed through the camera's field of view. That is different from an authorized user entering a plate number or vehicle description into the system and actively searching for matching records.

What might a search leave behind?

Depending on the system configuration and the agency's practices, a search may generate audit information showing that an authorized user accessed the system, what kind of search was performed, when it happened, and which account performed it.

The exact records available can vary by agency and by the technology being used. Flockit.info does not currently have enough evidence to promise that every community maintains the same audit records or that those records are always available to the public.

What can you ask for?

If you want to know whether your plate was searched, you can ask the agency that operates or uses the system whether it maintains audit logs, search-history records, access logs, or other records showing searches conducted through the platform.

Be specific about the plate number, the time period you're asking about, and the kind of activity you want identified. A narrow request is usually easier for an agency to process than a broad request for everything connected to the system.

What if your vehicle was only recorded?

A recorded sighting may exist even if nobody ever searched for you. That is one reason the distinction matters. The system can collect information automatically, while a later search is a separate action performed by a user.

What if the answer is unclear?

Ask what records exist, how long they are retained, whether user searches are logged, and whether the agency can identify who accessed the system and when. If the agency says no responsive records exist, keep that response with your request so the record is clear.
