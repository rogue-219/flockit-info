WHY SHOULD I CARE?

Maybe you don't care that a camera reads your plate.

That's fair. Your license plate is already visible when you drive down the street. The bigger question is what happens after a camera reads it.

“Why stand we here idle?”

Patrick Henry · 1775 speech, recorded later

So what happens after the camera reads your plate?

The picture is only the start. A record can include where your car was seen, when it was there, and details about the vehicle.

That record can be stored. It can be searched later. And depending on the rules, people outside your local police department may be able to search it too.

So the question isn't really whether a camera can see your license plate.

The question is what people are allowed to do with the information after they have it.

Would any of this matter to you?

Who can search for your car?

Is it only your local police department? Can police in another city or state search too? See what these systems can do.

Do they need a reason?

What does an officer have to enter before searching for a vehicle? Is there a case number? Is the reason recorded? See the policies and records we've collected.

How far back can they look?

A camera sees your car for a moment. A stored record can be searched later. Learn why storage changes the question.

Who checks the searches?

Rules only matter if someone checks whether people follow them. Who audits the system? How often? What happens when someone breaks the rules? See what we've found in your community.

Who makes the rules?

Your community can decide what rules belong around the system, how it is used, and what happens when those rules are broken. See what you can do.

You don't have to be against the cameras to care about the rules.

These systems can help police do their jobs. That doesn't mean there shouldn't be limits on how they are used.

You can support law enforcement and still ask who can search the system, why they can search it, how long the information is kept, who it is shared with, and who checks that the rules are followed.

That's what Flockit.info is here to help you find out.
