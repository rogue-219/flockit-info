Primary records come first.

Whenever possible, we rely on original government records and documents from the companies providing the technology. That includes contracts, amendments, ordinances, purchase records, policies, invoices, meeting materials, and public-records responses.

Why the source matters.

Surveillance debates can become opinion-heavy very quickly. The most useful starting point is often much simpler: what does the contract actually say, what did the government approve, what did it cost, what data can be collected, how long is it kept, and who can access it?

Community records.

Records tied to a specific city or agency will be organized with that community's profile so residents can see the source documents alongside the facts drawn from them.

What we're still collecting.

Some records are still being requested, reviewed, or verified. When information is incomplete, we'll say so rather than fill in the gaps.
